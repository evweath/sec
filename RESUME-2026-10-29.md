## What was confirmed on 2026-10-28

### Amazon failure is local Little Snitch filtering

- The available Little Snitch export has explicit Safari deny rules for `www.amazon.com` on TCP and UDP port 443, marked `[ls-week-review] doubt-approved->deny 2026-09-28`.
- Other Safari denies affect Amazon images, payments, `unagi.amazon.com`, and `s3.amazonaws.com`. The rule source for the primary Amazon domain rules is `ls-week-review.py`, not `limactl`.
- At 18:00:29, Little Snitch recorded Safari DNS flows for `www.amazon.com`; Safari immediately failed with `NSURLErrorDomain Code=-1005` and POSIX error 57, `Socket is not connected`.
- There were 37 Safari page-load errors in the examined 11-minute unified-log window.
- All 20 repeated `curl` probes to `www.amazon.com` returned HTTP 202, with gateway and internet pings also passing. `amazon.com` and `aws.amazon.com` worked too. This was process-specific filtering, not a general Amazon, DNS, route, or Wi-Fi outage.
- `limactl` was not running. No evidence tied it to this failure.

### Separate automatic-deny risk for other server connections

- `evw-security-system.py` runs `sentinel-deny-sync` every 300 seconds (`security-system.json`).
- That job invokes `ls-sentinel-deny.py`, which turns mac-sentinel connection warnings into any-process, per-IP/per-port Little Snitch denies.
- The available model contained 118 Amazon-related automatic sentinel denies. These rules can interrupt rotating Amazon/CDN and other server connections across applications.
- Example from 18:02: `scan-2026-10-28/ls-sentinel-deny-1793228530.md` added an any-process deny for a WebKit-observed Verizon address. This confirms the automatic job was active during the investigation.
- The automatic job is a credible contributor to broader connection drops, but it was not proven to explain every individual disconnect.
